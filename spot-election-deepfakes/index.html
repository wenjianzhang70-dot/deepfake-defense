How to Spot Election Deepfakes: A Voter's Quick Guide (2026)

Updated October 19, 2026 · 5 min read
This guide is strictly non-partisan. It covers techniques used against all candidates and voters, regardless of party. Our goal is voter protection, not political persuasion.

Ahead of the 2026 U.S. midterm elections, the FBI warned that scammers are impersonating political candidates and campaigns — using AI-generated voices, images, and written messages to steal money or personal information. The schemes borrow candidates' names, logos, and slogans to look legitimate.

3 election scams to watch for

  1. "Your registration is at risk" messages. Texts and emails pushing you to fix your registration through a link — the link leads to a fake page harvesting personal data.
  2. Fake donation requests. Mimicking real campaign outreach, pressuring quick donations. Some falsely promise your donation "grows" or gets returned — real donations never work that way.
  3. Calls from "the election office." Asking for ID numbers, banking details, or ballot IDs. Voice cloning is more accessible than ever: a familiar-sounding voice is no longer proof of who's calling. (The FCC made AI-generated voices in robocalls illegal back in 2024, after cloned-voice calls targeted New Hampshire primary voters — the abuse continues anyway.)

How to verify before you trust

  • Donations: go to the candidate's official website directly (type the URL yourself). Independently verify any donation request before responding.
  • Registration/voting info: use your state's official board of elections site or vote.gov — never a link from an unsolicited message.
  • Viral clips: use the SIFT method — Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace to the original context. Check Politifact, Snopes, FactCheck.org, or Reuters Fact Check.
  • Deepfake tells: unnatural blinking, lip-sync lag, lighting mismatches, and emotional mismatch still catch many fakes — see our video-call detection guide for the full checklist.
The one rule: verify through official channels, every time. A polished message or familiar voice is not proof. When in doubt, close the message and start from a source you typed yourself.
