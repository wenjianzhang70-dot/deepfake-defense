INTERPOL's 3 Rules Against Deepfake Scams (2026 Campaign)

Updated October 19, 2026 · 4 min read

On October 18, 2026, INTERPOL — the world's largest police organization — launched a public awareness campaign about AI deepfake scams, announcing it from Singapore. The message is blunt: fake faces and cloned voices are no longer aimed at celebrities. They're aimed at ordinary people.

In a fitting twist, the campaign's three short videos are themselves AI-generated — demonstrating exactly how synthetic media can trick you.

The 3 rules: wait, validate, don't take the bait

INTERPOL's advice for anyone who gets a suspicious call or video:

  1. Wait. Scams run on urgency. A pause breaks the script.
  2. Validate. Verify through a separate channel — call back on a known number, message on an internal system.
  3. Don't take the bait. Don't send money, share codes, or click links under pressure.

Why police are warning everyone now

Toshinobu Yasuhira, director of the INTERPOL Innovation Centre, says AI is "wiping out the classic signs of a scam" — spelling mistakes, off-brand logos, clumsy formatting. Generative AI tools no longer need much skill or money; with a small amount of content found online, criminals can convincingly copy a real person's face or voice.

The scams now reaching everyday people include investment fraud, impersonation, romance scams, sextortion, and disinformation — a video call from a "colleague" asking for a bank transfer, a panicked call from a "family member" in trouble, an online relationship that feels genuine but isn't.
